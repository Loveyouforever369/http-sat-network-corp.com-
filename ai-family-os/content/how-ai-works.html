The one-minute picture

Examples in. A pattern-finder. A best guess out.

Every AI you use follows this path. It learned from a mountain of examples, squeezed them into a "pattern engine," and now it predicts the most likely next piece — word, pixel, or sound.

The three things to know

Understand these and you "get" AI.

1. It predicts, it doesn't "know"

It picks the most likely next word from patterns it learned — like the world's best autocomplete. There's no understanding underneath, just very good guessing.

2. Training ≠ using

Learning the patterns (training) happened once, on huge data. When you chat with it, it's not learning from you — it's running what it already learned.

3. It can be confidently wrong

Because it's guessing, it sometimes invents things that sound right ("hallucinations"). That's why we always check facts against a real source.

Where it shines vs. where it slips

Use it for the right jobs.

✅ Great at

  • First drafts — emails, posts, plans
  • Summarizing long stuff fast
  • Explaining things simply
  • Brainstorming options
  • Turning a mess into structure

    • ⛔ Be careful with

  • Exact facts, dates, numbers (verify!)
  • Anything legal, medical, financial
  • Private data — don't paste secrets
  • "It said so" as proof
  • Letting it act with no human check
    • How to get great results

    The prompt formula that just works.

    You don't need tricks. You need to tell it four things — who to be, what to do, how to format it, and the limits.

    [Role] + [Task] + [Format] + [Constraints]

    e.g. "You're a friendly real-estate expert. Write 5 email subject lines for a first-time buyer. Numbered list. Under 50 characters each, no hype."
    Guardian's note: this is a deliberately simple picture — under the hood there's real math (neural networks, tokens, weights). But for actually using AI well, "examples in → pattern engine → best guess out, so verify it" is the mental model that matters. We'd rather you understand it truly than sound fancy.
